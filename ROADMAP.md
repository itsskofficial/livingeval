# Roadmap

What v0.1 deliberately does not do, and what would come next.

The list matters as much as the feature list. A tool that measures whether your
measurement is worth anything loses the argument the moment it becomes a platform.

---

## What used to be out of scope, and now ships

The v0.1 roadmap listed a web UI, a database, embeddings and fine-tuning as
indefinitely out of scope. All four now ship, because the argument for excluding them
was weaker than it looked — a measurement nobody can see is a measurement nobody acts
on, and "we left it out on principle" is not a principle.

What survived the rethink is the *shape* each of them took:

| shipped | and the constraint kept |
|---|---|
| a local platform | binds localhost, no auth, one SQLite file; an import-linter contract stops the library ever depending on it |
| a store | stdlib SQLite by default, same schema on Postgres, no ORM and no migrations |
| embeddings | first-class, but the default stays tf-idf and `compare-spaces` measures whether that costs you anything |
| fine-tuning | rung 5, opt-in, flagged `is_diagnostic=False`, with a note wherever its number appears |

## Still deliberately out of scope

- **Multi-tenancy, auth, hosting.** The platform is a control surface for your machine.
  Adding tenants is the point at which this competes with four funded companies instead
  of measuring something none of them measure. See [DECISIONS.md #25](DECISIONS.md).
- **Running your agent.** `livingeval` reads traces. What produced them is your problem
  and there are better tools for it.
- **A general assertion library.** `assert "sorry" not in response` is not this
  library's job.
- **A scheduler.** Analyses run on request. Coverage over 20k traces takes seconds; a
  scheduler would add configuration surface and a class of "why is this number stale"
  bug in exchange for nothing at this size.

## Next, in rough order of value

### 1. The real-judge ladder

The single most important experiment and the first one to run. Point `scorer.ladder` at
`judge.openai("gpt-4o-mini")` and `judge.anthropic("claude-sonnet-4-5")` over a real
public agent-trace corpus and publish the rungs. Everything in the current audit exists
to make this number mean something; nothing in the audit substitutes for it.

Wanted: κ per rung, per traffic cluster, with the two judges' κ against each other as a
ceiling — you cannot expect a cheap scorer to reproduce a judge better than a second
frontier judge does.

### 2. A real trace corpus

The synthetic generators are the right instrument for Findings 1 and 2 and the wrong
one for external credibility on Finding 3. Candidates: an openly licensed agent-trace
dataset, or a corpus generated by running a real agent over a public task set and
publishing the traces alongside the analysis.

### 3. Human-labelled validation sets

`judge.validate` currently has ground truth only where a generator wrote it. A small,
carefully labelled set (200–400 traces, two annotators, inter-annotator κ published)
would turn `UNVALIDATED` from a warning into a workflow.

### 4. The embedding comparison, on real traffic

Shipped as `livingeval compare-spaces`, and on the bundled corpus the spread is 0.042 —
the cheap default costs nothing there. What is missing is the same measurement on **real
traffic**, where synonymy matters more than it does in a generator that composes intents
from fixed vocabularies. If the spread turns out large on real data, the default needs
revisiting and every cross-project comparison needs the representation pinned harder
than it is now.

### 5. Multiclass and graded verdicts

Everything is binary today. Rubrics that score 1–5, or emit a failure taxonomy, are
common and need: κ generalised to weighted agreement, McNemar generalised to
Stuart–Maxwell, and a gate that can distinguish "worse" from "different".

### 6. Streaming / incremental coverage

Coverage is currently a batch computation over a window. A running estimate that
updates per trace would make it a dashboard metric rather than a CI metric, which is
where it belongs — it is the earliest signal and the cheapest to compute
([Finding 1](README.md)).

### 7. Cost-aware suite selection

Given a review budget of *n* cases, which *n* traces maximise expected detection power?
`mine.propose` currently does greedy k-centre over blind clusters, which is a
reasonable heuristic and demonstrably not optimal — the audit shows 40 confirmed cases
per window recovering coverage from 0.43 to 0.61. The optimisation version is a real
problem with a measurable objective, and the objective already exists in
`power.expected_power`.

### 8. Per-turn rather than per-trace

Scoring is trace-level. Turn-level scoring needs a `Turn`-scoped judge protocol and a
coverage notion over turns. Wanted by anyone doing long agentic sessions, and the YC
survey's "nobody runs per-turn evaluation" is literally about this.

## Known limitations in v0.1

- **Clustering is KMeans with `k` by silhouette.** Fine for reporting, wrong if you
  believe the clusters are real structure. They are a summarisation device; the
  decision-relevant number, coverage, is per trace.
- **Power simulations assume independent per-case flake.** Real instability is
  correlated — a flaky retriever fails a whole topic at once. Correlated flake would
  widen every interval in Finding 2.
- **`expected_power` truncates to the top clusters by traffic share.** The truncation
  and the share of traffic it covers are both reported, but it is a truncation.
- **The OTel adapter is lossy in documented ways.** Concurrent tool spans get a stable
  but arbitrary order; only the first LLM span's prompt is expanded.
- **No Windows-specific path handling has been stress-tested** beyond the development
  machine, which is Windows.
- **The platform holds one fitted scorer per process.** Refitting blocks the request
  that triggered it. Fine for a local control surface; wrong for anything shared.
- **Fine-tuning trains one model per CV fold**, so a five-fold ladder with the rung
  enabled trains five times. Correct, and slow enough that it is opt-in.
- **The Fireworks backend writes the dataset and stops.** Their fine-tuning surface has
  moved more than once; rather than pin to a version that will break, it reports the
  exact `firectl` commands to run. Documented rather than pretended.
